# Phone/pocket ASR search, 2026-10-02

## Result and Scope

The development-selected recipe is **Qwen3-ASR-1.7B from the Mega-ASR bundle,
adapter disabled, beam 2, English forced, BF16, lossless mono 16 kHz input**.
Unfiltered audio won the preprocessing search. This is the best tested recipe
under the predefined development objective, not a proof of a global minimum WER.

Independent held-out phone and obstructed-microphone recordings show a substantial
improvement over the tested Whisper large-v3 baseline. The private target has no
verified reference: neither disagreement with its old Whisper transcript nor a
more fluent new transcript establishes its true WER.

Target: `/home/lewis/Dev/diarise/Recording_1828.mp3`, approximately **5 h 49 min**.
Despite the extension, its container is MP4/M4A and its codec is AAC-LC, mono,
16 kHz. SHA-256:
`b6435e650a61cdc49864c116f86e12a77b11a55a9fcf13584e52dd8a2a23e40c`.
The source and the existing transcripts are not overwritten.

## Data and Scoring

| Corpus | Development used for tuning | Held out | What it tests |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Voices-in-the-Wild | 22 clips | 123 clips | Real noisy, obstructed and mixed English audio |
| Krisp phone calls | 14 clips | 24 clips | Actual mobile recordings, including street/walking and car conditions |
| BERSt non-shouted recordings | 84 clips | 353 clips | Actual pocket, bag, covered-microphone and distant phone recordings |

The mixed development cohort has 1,518 normalized reference words; BERSt has 348.
The held-out mixed cohort has 4,069 words; BERSt has 1,474. BERSt's actual-pocket
held-out subset has **53 clips and 227 words**. It is a short prompted-speech test,
not a continuous pocket-walking conversation.

Krisp and BERSt use speaker-group-disjoint partitions. Voices-in-the-Wild uses
source/index-disjoint partitions; speaker independence is not verified. BERSt
audio was deduplicated by SHA-256; two contradictory-reference duplicate pairs
were excluded. Selection minimized the unweighted mean of mixed-cohort WER and
BERSt WER, so the longer phone-call references cannot dominate the pocket test.
Holdout scores were not used to reselect the primary configuration.

WER uses `EnglishTextNormalizer` from `whisper-normalizer` and `jiwer`, with
substitutions, deletions and insertions summed against reference word counts.
It ignores punctuation and casing and normalizes numbers/spelling. **Punctuation
quality is not independently measured here.** No references, technical phrase
lists, repository normalizer or UK spelling conversion are supplied to a model.
Krisp's reference covers the primary speaker, which is not equivalent to a
verbatim transcript of every background voice in the private recording.

"Raw" means the model's native API transcript before additional project-level
editing, not an untouched decoder-token stream. Qwen's supplied parser itself
collapses very long character/pattern repetitions. Generation caps and EOS are
therefore audited separately; a short API string can conceal a capped decoder.

All run in BF16. Decoder sampling is disabled except Whisper's documented
temperature fallback. Whisper uses native Transformers, temperature sequence
0/0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8/1, previous-text conditioning, timestamp-driven generation,
compression threshold 2.4, log-probability threshold -1 and no-speech threshold
0.6. **This is not an exact runtime/configuration reproduction of the existing
faster-whisper/CTranslate2 service or the old target's batched transcript.**

ARK and Hojo clips longer than 30 seconds are divided at low-energy boundaries,
without overlap or dropping source samples. Hojo uses its source implementation
with BF16 casting/autocast and a SoundFile decoding adapter, but uses the newer
ROCm Torch runtime rather than its upstream older Torch pin. VibeVoice uses its
official 24 kHz level-normalizing processor and 2,048-token structured generation.
Qwen, ARK, Hojo and MOSS use a 512-token generation cap, audited per chunk.

| DeepFilterNet3 with 12 dB attenuation limit | 2.37% | 15.80% |

An additional **144 conditional rules** selected filtering using only the
original waveform's low-frequency power ratio and absolute low-band level.
None improved the two-cohort development objective. Raw audio was frozen before
holdout. This does not establish that filtering can never help another recording;
it does establish that blindly filtering this pipeline is not a validated gain.

## Held-out Results

| Corpus/subset | Whisper beam 2 | Whisper beam 5 | Frozen Qwen beam 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Real phone calls, 24 clips | 3.82% | 3.72% | **1.50%** |
| Voices-in-the-Wild, 123 clips | 6.00% | 5.72% | **5.53%** |
| All BERSt obstruction conditions, 353 clips | 27.07% | 27.95% | **19.13%** |
| Actual pocket subset, 53 clips | 32.16% | 32.60% | **14.10%** |

Relative to Whisper beam 2, this is approximately 61% fewer phone-call errors,
29% fewer obstruction errors and 56% fewer actual-pocket errors. Paired bootstrap
resampling of speaker/source groups (5,000 draws, fixed seed) gives Qwen-minus-
Whisper WER differences in percentage points:

| Corpus | Difference | 95% percentile interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Krisp phone calls | -2.32 | -3.61 to -1.60 |
| BERSt obstruction | -7.94 | -11.26 to -4.84 |
| Voices-in-the-Wild | -0.47 | -1.51 to +0.49 |

The independent phone/obstruction improvements survive this uncertainty check.
The Voices-in-the-Wild difference does not. The nearby Qwen greedy and Mega
router-greedy configurations also performed well: mixed WER 3.66%/3.44% and
BERSt WER 18.59%/18.66%, versus the frozen beam-2 recipe's 3.61%/19.13%.
Therefore beam 2 is **not** a uniquely proven universal winner, and it was not
changed after looking at holdout scores.

A development-selected Qwen + Whisper + VibeVoice consensus was also frozen and
tested. It improved mixed held-out WER from 3.61% to 3.29%, but worsened BERSt
from 19.13% to 19.47%; the two-cohort mean did not improve. It is not promoted as
a reliable general improvement. Qwen and Mega are correlated voters, not two
independent confirmations of a word.

## Target Handling and Outputs

The original file is decoded once to PCM mono 16 kHz without lossy re-encoding.
The first pass uses Silero VAD threshold 0.25, 150 ms minimum speech, 700 ms minimum
silence and 400 ms padding. Nearby spans are grouped only when their full original
interval is at most 45 seconds; internal pauses are preserved. This produces 748
initial groups covering about 3 h 14 min.

An audit of 20 uncovered regions produced plausible speech in some VAD-rejected
intervals. Treating all rejected regions as silence would therefore be unsafe.
The completion pass transcribes the entire uncovered source, including short
gaps, and validates source-sample coverage before exporting a complete version.
Rejected-region results are retained and flagged for review, not asserted to be
verified speech. Nothing is silently removed by an LLM cleanup or majority vote.

The old transcript contains obvious repeated-text loops around 01:40:00. The
quiet-region audit does not reproduce those long loops, but removing a loop alone
does not prove that every replacement word is correct. Separate 18-region Qwen,
Mega-router, Whisper and VibeVoice samples retain model disagreements for review;
VibeVoice also produced repetition loops on this target and is not a safe default.

The completed first pass took 790.88 seconds of measured inference; the uncovered
pass took 448.73 seconds. Together they cover **335,361,024 samples / 20,960.064
seconds**, in **1,550 non-overlapping chunks**, with **zero missing samples**.
The 802 uncovered chunks account for 9,339.808 seconds (44.56% of the source).
These are model-call timings, not download/setup or complete wall-clock timings.

Sixteen uncovered chunks hit a generation warning. A reference-free greedy retry
reached complete generation for six; ten remain explicitly flagged. EOS is an
execution check, not a guarantee of correct recognition. The original predictions
and both failed/successful retry text remain in the evidence; no incomplete retry
is silently substituted. `benchmarks/retry_target_generation.py` implements this
guard, separately from the frozen public benchmark configuration.

- `Recording_1828_recommended_20261002.txt`: reader-friendly version that marks the ten unfinished decodes instead of asserting their incomplete output as speech.
- `Recording_1828_recommended_20261002_timestamped.txt`: the same guarded reader view with source times and LOW-VAD review flags.
- `Recording_1828_fullsource_20261002_recovered.txt`: complete-source API transcript with the six completed retries.
- `Recording_1828_fullsource_20261002_recovered_review_timestamped.txt`: the recommended review view, with source times, sample intervals, LOW-VAD and generation flags.
- `Recording_1828_fullsource_20261002_recovered.json`: all segment metadata and original/retry evidence.
- `Recording_1828_fullsource_20261002_recovered.coverage.json`: source hashes, exact coverage and recovery counts.
- `Recording_1828_fullsource_20261002.*`: original full-source beam-2 results, retained separately and unmodified.

Private transcripts are ignored by the diarise repository and kept out of this
project's public experiment documentation. The remote experiment directory is
restricted to its owner. The existing recording and old transcript are unchanged.

The unguarded Mega-ASR router was also tried on the target, activating its adapter
only where the saved degradation score was at least 0.5. It was **rejected early**:
**45 of 66 completed routed chunks** had capped/unfinished generation, versus
**zero on those same 66 chunks with Qwen base**. The unfinished 221-chunk trial
was interrupted after preserving its outputs; it is not a completed full-target
WER comparison. This degradation router is not a speech detector, and activating
its adapter over very quiet audio was unsafe in this recording. Its failed
predictions and `target-router-rejected.json` are retained, not substituted into
the recommended transcript. There is no completed router-alternative transcript.

The reader view does not change the recorded ASR results. It introduces explicit
review markers for the ten known unfinished decodes; these markers are not words
claimed to occur in the audio and are not used to calculate a target WER.

## Reproduction and Audit Artifacts

`benchmarks/` contains the resumable model grid, preprocessing/consensus controls,
target extraction/rendering and offline regression tests. The isolated environment
descriptions are `benchmarks/runtime/pyproject.toml` and
`benchmarks/enhancement/pyproject.toml`; Hojo additionally needs the documented
vendor dependencies rather than changes to the production environment.

Local score tables and frozen configuration artifacts are in
`.tmp/noisy-asr-20261002/`; `all-scores.csv` includes the detailed run/category
scores. The complete 25 MiB result archive is in its `remote-results/` child,
including public reference/prediction pairs, private target results, configuration
fingerprints, generation warnings and the rejected router audit. Three preliminary
runs use a different 36-clip subset and must not be
compared with the later two-cohort grid. Their IDs are `base-raw-b1-dev`,
`mega-auto-raw-b1-dev` and `whisper-b2-raw-dev`.

New runs record model revision metadata, source-audio SHA-256, configuration,
helper-code hash and per-clip seed. Resumption refuses changed models, audio or
settings. Earlier deterministic development caches predate the complete sidecar;
early Whisper temperature-fallback runs were not per-clip seeded. Held-out Whisper
runs and new target runs use stable per-ID seeds. All numerical results remain
specific to these model/runtime/audio/reference/normalizer combinations.

ROCm fused attention hung on an early long clip, so subsequent GPU experiments
use process-local math SDPA rather than changing system-wide settings. Mega's
adapter switch resets pristine BF16 weights before applying a delta, avoiding
rounding drift from repeated BF16 add/subtract route changes. Router classification
covers every 30-second window rather than truncating long audio to its positional
buffer.

Verification: **72 offline regression tests passed**, Python compilation passed,
and `git diff --check` passed. Tests cover source/speaker isolation, waveform and
interval conservation, adapter reset, resume fingerprints, generation recovery,
reader warnings and CPU/GPU workflow gating. They do not certify the accuracy of
unverified target words. No unrelated changes were reverted or committed.
